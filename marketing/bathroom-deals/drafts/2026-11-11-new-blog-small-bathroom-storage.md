# Small Bathroom Storage Ideas That Don't Eat Floor Space

In a small bathroom, every bit of floor you can see makes the room feel bigger, and every bit you cover makes it feel smaller. So the trick with storage isn't finding more of it. It's getting it off the floor and onto the walls, where it works just as hard and takes up nothing underfoot.

Below are the wall-mounted options that earn their place in a small bathroom or ensuite, with real products from our range, prices as at November 2026, and the fixing details to sort before the walls are sheeted.

## Start with what you actually need to store

Before buying anything, sort what's in the bathroom now into **daily items** (toothbrushes, skincare, soap, shampoo), **weekly items** (spare towels, cleaning products, hair tools) and **rarely used items** (bulk toilet paper, spares), which can go to the hall cupboard.

Most small bathrooms go wrong because daily items end up on the vanity top and weekly items end up on the floor. Match each pile to a type of wall storage: open shelves and hooks for daily things, closed cabinetry for weekly things.

## 1. A wall-hung vanity with drawers, not doors

The vanity is the biggest storage piece in most bathrooms, so it's the first decision. A wall-hung vanity leaves the floor visible underneath, which makes a narrow room read wider and makes mopping much easier. We covered the full comparison in [Wall-Hung vs Freestanding Vanities: Which Suits a Small Bathroom?](/wall-hung-vs-freestanding-vanities-which-suits-a-small-bathroom/), so here's the storage angle.

**Drawers beat doors in a small room.** With doors you crouch, reach past the waste pipe and dig. With drawers, the whole contents slide out to you, and you don't need clear floor in front for a door to swing.

The [Nimis 900 Wall Hung Vanity](/product/nimis-900-wall-hung-vanity-with-matte-white-solid-surface-top/) ($965) is a good example. It's 900mm long, 460mm deep and 570mm high, with two soft-close drawers, a natural beechwood veneer front and a matte white solid surface top that suits an above-counter or semi-inset basin (basin not included). Its product page lists a 5-year warranty.

Things to check before you commit:

**Height and position.** Put it where you can reach it without stepping into the full spray, usually on the wall beside the shower outlet rather than under it. ## Fixing into studs or noggins: the part to sort before the plasterboard goes on

This is the most important section if you're mid-renovation.

Loaded wall-hung cabinets are heavy, especially a vanity with someone leaning on it. Plasterboard anchors alone aren't the right fixing; cabinets need to be fixed back to the frame.

- **Ask for noggins.** Before sheeting, have your builder or carpenter add horizontal noggins between the studs where the vanity and tall boy will hang, and photograph the frame so you can find them later.
- **Do the same for rails and hooks.** Towels get yanked, and a noggin behind each rail stops it working loose.
- **Already tiled?** Use a stud finder and fixings rated for the wall and load. If there's no framing where you need it, ask a carpenter or installer before drilling.
- **Keep clear of pipes and cables.** Photos of the open frame taken before sheeting show you where they run; if you don't have any, check with your plumber or electrician before drilling.

## FAQ

**What's the best storage for a very small bathroom?**
A wall-hung vanity with drawers, plus a narrow wall-hung tall boy if there's a gap beside it. Add hooks and a double towel rail for the things you use every day. Keeping the floor clear does more for a small room than any single piece.

**Can I put a shower shelf on an existing tiled wall?**
Yes, but drilling goes through the waterproofing, so each fixing must be sealed properly. If you're unsure, get your tiler or installer to do it.

**Are Bathroom Deals vanities and tall boys delivered?**
No. Vanities and tall boys are collection only from our Acacia Ridge warehouse in Brisbane. Accessories such as shelves, hooks and rails are sent Australia-wide with tracking.

**Do I need a plumber to install a vanity?**
Yes. In Queensland and most other states, the water and waste connections must be done by a licensed plumber. A carpenter or handy installer can hang the cabinet, but the plumbing is licensed work.
