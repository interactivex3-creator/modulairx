# Bathroom Deals — Marketing Baseline Report

**Prepared:** 8 Nov 2026 · **Period:** 1 Aug – 8 Nov 2026 · **Sources:** WooCommerce REST API (bathroomdeals.au), WordPress, Google Merchant Center via Windsor.ai, Google Analytics 4 via Site Kit.

---

## TL;DR
1. **Real traffic is small.** GA4 shows 1,730 sessions in 90 days, but about 1,000 of them are overseas bots. **Real Australian traffic is about 580 sessions (~45 a week).** Organic search is the best real channel.
1a. **~~The cart is the main drop-off point~~ (corrected: the add-to-cart data was bot traffic).** 332 of the 344 `add_to_cart` events came from bots in two bursts (17–22 Sep and 30 Oct–2 Nov), and only 10 came from Australia. Real shoppers who add to cart mostly go on to buy (≈7 add-to-cart users → 8 checkout users → 5 purchases). **The problem is too few visitors, not a broken checkout.**
1b. **Enquiries outnumber orders about 5 to 1:** 28 form submissions against 6 orders. The quote path matters.
1c. **Google Shopping:** 223 products approved, and 83 clicks in 90 days. Search Console is connected but returns no data (see §2b).
2. **Sales are very low:** 6 orders since 1 Aug, 2 of which were refunded. Net sales were **$967**, and only 4 orders were kept.
3. **Every sale was a discounted product**, mostly the Murcia range at 50–70% off.
4. **Fix the catalogue before spending on promotion.** **20 of the 24 Neston drain products are out of stock**, including the most-viewed one. 26 products are out of stock in total, and a few warranty claims contradict each other.
5. **Compliance risk:** 27 products show large "was/now" discounts (up to 70% off). Under Australian Consumer Law, each "was" price needs evidence that the product genuinely sold at that price.

### Add-to-cart bot activity (investigated 8 Nov)
| Check | Finding |
|---|---|
| Channel | 332 of 344 `add_to_cart` events were "Direct", 7 "Unassigned", and 5 Organic Search |
| Users | **341 users for 344 events**: one add-to-cart each, then gone |
| Country | US 47, Singapore 35, Brazil 33, Indonesia 25, Hong Kong 24, Japan 23… **Australia only 10 (7 users)** |
| Timing | **Two bursts only:** 17–22 Sep (238 events) and 30 Oct–2 Nov (59). Zero on every other day. |

**Conclusion:** these were automated add-to-cart bots, not shoppers. This kind of activity often comes before **card-testing fraud** (bots trying stolen card numbers at checkout). It also fills the **Abandoned Cart Lite** plugin with fake carts, which skews its recovery stats. Abandoned-cart emails go to fake or harvested addresses if the bots enter an email, which can damage the domain's sending reputation.

**Once real shoppers commit, they buy. The constraint is the number of real visitors, and getting them to a product they want.** The earlier "cart drop-off / shipping shock" theory is withdrawn.

### Actions for the bot traffic
1. **WooPayments:** check that fraud and card-testing protection is on (Payments › Settings › Fraud protection).
2. **Block or slow the bots:** LiteSpeed Cache / your host / Cloudflare bot-fight mode; rate-limit `?add-to-cart=` and `/wc/store/v1/cart` requests; optionally add CAPTCHA or Turnstile at checkout.
3. **Abandoned Cart Lite:** delete the fake carts from 17–22 Sep and 30 Oct–2 Nov. Set it to ignore guest carts that have no email, and check its email log to see whether reminders went to junk addresses.
4. **GA4:** add a filter or comparison that excludes these bursts, or limits reports to Australia, so reports reflect real shoppers.

## 4. Compliance flag (for `bd-compliance-reviewer`)
The About page promises: *"Our sale prices are measured against what the product genuinely sold for."* Under Australian Consumer Law, the ACCC treats "was" or RRP prices as misleading unless the product genuinely sold at that price for a reasonable period beforehand.

**Action:** for each of the 27 discounted products, confirm there's evidence the product sold at the "was" price (order history, or the dates the price was live). Where there's no evidence, remove the strikethrough price and show the current price only. Brighton Laundry Package (ID 985) and its "RRP $1,586" wording are the highest risk.
